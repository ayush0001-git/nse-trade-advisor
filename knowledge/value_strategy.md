# VALUE INVESTING STRATEGY

## DEFINITION
Buy stocks trading below their intrinsic value. Popularized by Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett. The edge: markets overreact to bad news, creating discounts for patient buyers.

## WHY IT WORKS
- **Mean reversion**: depressed valuations eventually recover
- **Behavioral bias**: investors extrapolate recent problems into perpetuity
- **Margin of safety**: buying below intrinsic value provides downside protection
- **Catalyst**: value unlocks via earnings recovery, buybacks, or M&A

## METRICS
- **P/E ratio**: < 15 (market), < 10 (deep value)
- **EV/EBITDA**: < 8 (market), < 5 (deep value)
- **Price/Book**: < 1.5 (market), < 1.0 (deep value)
- **Free Cash Flow yield**: > 8% (FCF/market cap)
- **ROE**: > 15% (quality value, not value traps)
- **Debt/Equity**: < 0.5 (avoid leveraged value traps)
- **PEG ratio**: < 1.0 (growth-adjusted value)

## ENTRY RULES
1. P/E < 15 AND P/B < 1.5
2. ROE > 15% (quality filter — avoid value traps)
3. Debt/Equity < 0.5
4. Free cash flow positive for 3 consecutive years
5. Stock is 30%+ below 52-week high (oversold)
6. Margin of safety > 25% (intrinsic value - market price / intrinsic value)

## EXIT RULES
- Price reaches intrinsic value (sell into strength)
- Fundamentals deteriorate (ROE drops below 10%, debt spikes)
- Time exit: 2 years (value can take time to unlock)
- Stop-loss: 20% below entry (wider than momentum — value is noisy)

## USED BY
- Warren Buffett (Berkshire Hathaway)
- Benjamin Graham (father of value investing)
- Seth Klarman (Baupost Group)
- Howard Marks (Oaktree — distressed value)

## VALUE TRAPS — RED FLAGS
- Declining revenue for 3+ quarters
- Accounting irregularities (watch footnotes)
- High insider selling
- Industry in structural decline
- Low ROIC (growth destroys value)
- Auditor changes (often signal problems)

## BACKTESTED EDGE
- Historical outperformance: 1-3% annually over growth
- Works best in recovery phases (after bear markets)
- Underperforms in bull markets (growth outperforms)
- Sharpe ratio: 0.4-0.7
- Long drawdowns (can underperform for 3-5 years)

## INDIAN MARKET ADAPTATION
- PSUs often trade at value discounts (HPCL, BPCL, Coal India)
- Cyclical stocks (metals, capital goods) swing between value and growth
- Watch for promoter pledging (red flag in India)
- Use consolidated financials (not standalone — Indian companies have many subsidiaries)

## COMMON MISTAKES
1. **Buying cheap without quality** — low P/E + low ROE = value trap
2. **Ignoring the balance sheet** — debt kills value stocks in downturns
3. **No catalyst** — value without a catalyst stays cheap forever
4. **Averaging down on deteriorating fundamentals** — sell if the thesis breaks
5. **Confusing cheap with valuable** — a stock at P/E 5 might be expensive if earnings are about to collapse
